# Data

- `upstream/<source>/` — other people's data, exactly as published, with its
  licence and notice, pinned in [upstream/sources.toml](upstream/sources.toml)
  by version and SHA-256. Never edit these files: corrections go in jespersen's
  own data, which overrides them.
- `derived/<language>/` — data jespersen generates from upstream data, committed
  so builds need no training step, and regenerated by a `mise` task named in
  each file's header. Derived data carries its source's licence and notice:
  - [derived/en-us/letters.tsv](derived/en-us/letters.tsv) — American English
    letter-to-sound rules learned from CMUdict (`mise run en:letters`; how well
    they read unseen words: `mise run en:letters:evaluate`).
  - [derived/zh/characters.tsv](derived/zh/characters.tsv) — each Chinese
    character's Mandarin reading on its own, from Unihan, whose archive is
    not committed (`mise run zh:characters -- path/to/Unihan_Readings.txt`).
  - [derived/da/nst.tsv](derived/da/nst.tsv) — Danish words, their part of
    speech and pronunciation from NST's lexicon (CC0), whose archive is not
    committed (`mise run data:nst:da -- path/to/dan030224NST.pron`).
  - [derived/sv/nst.tsv](derived/sv/nst.tsv) — the Swedish words NST's
    transcribers wrote by hand outside the lexicons NST bought (CC0; decision
    42), from the same kind of archive (`mise run data:nst:sv -- path/to/swe030224NST.pron`).
- `<language>/` — jespersen's own data, dedicated to the public domain (CC0) with
  the rest of the repository:
  - [en-us/lexicon.tsv](en-us/lexicon.tsv) — American English entries that
    replace CMUdict's, each with its reason.
  - [en-gb/lexicon.tsv](en-gb/lexicon.tsv) — British English words the rules
    cannot derive from the American forms (schedule, tomato, laboratory),
    written as the American phonemes the rules turn into them.
  - [zh/lexicon.tsv](zh/lexicon.tsv) — Mandarin words and characters whose
    reading replaces ipa-dict's and Unihan's (重庆, 得 on its own), each with
    its reason.
  - [zh/words.tsv](zh/words.tsv) — everyday Mandarin words the sources lack
    or give without their neutral tones (东西 dōngxi), with Traditional forms
    where a character has several.
  - [es/lexicon.tsv](es/lexicon.tsv) — Spanish words the spelling rules read
    wrongly, respelt as they are said (méxico → méjico), each with its reason.
  - [it/words.tsv](it/words.tsv) — Italian words respelt with the accents
    dictionaries use, for the stress and the open or closed e and o the
    rules would miss (tavolo → tàvolo, bene → bène, sole → sóle).
  - [tr/words.tsv](tr/words.tsv) — Turkish words whose stress is not on the
    last syllable, written with the stress mark.
  - [pt/words.tsv](pt/words.tsv) — Portuguese words respelt for the open e
    and o the spelling does not show (ela → éla, porta → pórta) and the x
    that is not ʃ (táxi → táksi).
  - [fr/words.tsv](fr/words.tsv) — French words respelt for the final
    consonants, vowels and digraphs the rules would miss (fils → fisse,
    femme → famme, ville → vile).
  - [fr/aspirated.txt](fr/aspirated.txt) — French words with an aspirated h,
    before which there is no liaison or elision (héros, haricot).
  - [cs/words.tsv](cs/words.tsv) — Czech loanwords whose d, t and n stay
    hard before i (univerzita → unyverzita).
  - [sk/words.tsv](sk/words.tsv) — the same for Slovak.
  - [sl/words.tsv](sl/words.tsv) — Slovenian words whose stress or open e and
    o the rules miss, accented as Slovenian dictionaries write them (žogo →
    žôgo, deset → desét).
  - [ro/words.tsv](ro/words.tsv) — Romanian words said with j (este → ieste,
    el → iel) or stressed where the rules would not put it, marked with an
    acute accent (așa → așá).
  - [ca/words.tsv](ca/words.tsv) — Catalan words whose open or closed vowel,
    stress or final r the rules miss, accented as Catalan writes them (tot →
    tót, cosa → còsa).
  - [de/words.tsv](de/words.tsv) — German words whose vowel length or stress
    the rules miss, and the old spelling's ß after a short vowel (Erde → Ehrde,
    daß → dass).
  - [nl/words.tsv](nl/words.tsv) — Dutch words whose stress the rules miss, or
    with an unstressed e said in full (waarom → waaróm, duizend → duizènd).
  - [af/words.tsv](af/words.tsv) — Afrikaans words whose stress the rules miss
    (kombuis → kombúis, vandag → vandág).
  - [sv/words.tsv](sv/words.tsv) — Swedish words whose short vowel or stress the
    rules miss (han → hann, miljon → miljón).
  - [nb/words.tsv](nb/words.tsv) — Norwegian Bokmål function words said otherwise
    than spelt (og → å, de → di).
  - [cy/words.tsv](cy/words.tsv) — Welsh words whose stress the rules miss
    (Cymraeg → Cymráeg).
  - [ga/words.tsv](ga/words.tsv) — Irish words whose stress or vowel the rules
    miss, as Munster says them (Sjoestedt, Kerry, 1931 and 1938): the adverbs
    and the forms of ag stressed on their second syllable (amach → aˈmach, acu
    → aˈcu) and a few words said otherwise than spelt (air → eir, is → ios).
  - [lv/words.tsv](lv/words.tsv) — Latvian loanwords whose o the rules would
    say as uo (opera → òpera, kino → kinō).
  - [lt/words.tsv](lt/words.tsv) — Lithuanian numbers whose standard stress is
    not on the last syllable but one (penki → penkì, dvylika → dvýlika).
  - [da/words.tsv](da/words.tsv) — Danish function words said otherwise than
    spelt, an apostrophe keeping a vowel short (og → o', det → de).
  - [ms/words.tsv](ms/words.tsv) — Malay words whose e is e (taling), not ə,
    respelt with é (meja → méja).
  - [ru/words.tsv](ru/words.tsv) — Russian words, word forms and stems with
    their stress, which the spelling does not show (молоко́, кни́г-n, стол+n),
    and words said otherwise than spelt (что → што).

Every source and its licence is listed in
[docs/data-sources.md](../docs/data-sources.md).
